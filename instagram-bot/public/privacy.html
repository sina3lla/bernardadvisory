2. What information do we process?

When you interact with the connected accounts, we receive information from Instagram through Meta’s API. Depending on your interaction and the enabled features, this includes:

  • Instagram account or sender identifiers, and usernames where provided; comment, message and media identifiers; and the text and related metadata of comments or messages supplied to the app.
  • Matched keywords or moderation rules, outgoing replies, send times, processing results, errors, and Meta API responses.
  • Technical records used to prevent duplicate replies and limit reply frequency, including sender-specific reply-template history and counters.

Incoming text is analysed to select a response or moderation action. Our normal event logs record identifiers, rule matches and outcomes rather than a complete incoming conversation transcript. API responses or error details may contain additional information. Outgoing DM templates used for a sender are stored in reply history.

When you visit this page, the hosting service processes connection information such as your IP address, request time, requested URL, browser information, referrer where supplied, and response status. This page does not use analytics, advertising trackers, external fonts or cookies of its own.

If you email us, we process your email address, correspondence and information needed to handle your request.

3. Why do we use this information?

We use the app to send requested information by DM when a comment matches a keyword, answer incoming DMs, post relevant public replies, and moderate comments by hiding or deleting content that matches configured rules. Which actions occur depends on the account’s enabled settings. We also use technical records to prevent repeated messages, enforce reply limits, troubleshoot failures and protect the service.

Routine communication, moderation and security rely on our legitimate interests under Article 6(1)(f) GDPR: responding efficiently, maintaining our account discussions and operating a reliable service. Where an inquiry concerns entering into or performing a contract with you, Article 6(1)(b) applies as appropriate. Handling statutory privacy requests relies on Article 6(1)(c). If a separate activity requires consent, we must obtain it before that activity; interacting with the bot is not blanket consent to unrelated marketing.

The automation uses configured rules and reply templates. We do not send your comments or DMs to external AI services, CRMs or spreadsheets, and we do not sell this data. You are not required to use the bot. Without the information needed to process an interaction, an automated reply may not be possible.

4. Who receives the information?

Hetzner Online GmbH provides our web hosting and email infrastructure and processes hosted data on our behalf. Hetzner states that its non-cloud hosting products store and process data within the EU. See Hetzner’s data protection information.

Meta/Instagram delivers interactions to the app and receives the reply or moderation requests required to operate it. Public replies are visible to people who can view the relevant Instagram content. Meta also processes data for its own platform purposes, separately from our app.

Meta operates internationally and may process data outside the EEA, including in the United States. Its applicable transfer mechanisms and how to obtain information about safeguards are explained in the Instagram Privacy Policy. This policy does not replace Meta’s policy.

5. How long do we keep it?

  • Bot event logs: records become eligible for removal after 30 days. A daily cleanup removes expired entries, normally within the following day.
  • Processing records: comment and action records are removed after 30 days from their last recorded update, on the next daily cleanup. Dated counters are cleared on a similar schedule using their UTC calendar date.
  • Sender-specific reply history: retained while the corresponding sender/rule remains active, then removed on the next daily cleanup after 30 days without a recorded send for that sender/rule. This history does not have a separate timestamp for every reply. Any undated, interrupted-send history receives a one-time 30-day expiry when first detected.
  • Hosting access and error logs: Hosting cleanup is configured to delete weblogs older than 7 days. These logs are separate from the bot cleanup, including older event copies previously written to PHP’s error log.
  • Backups: Hetzner’s standard web-hosting backups are retained for up to 14 days. Deleted active data can remain in those backups until they expire. Restoring a backup requires reapplying relevant deletions.
  • Privacy correspondence: kept while needed to resolve and document your request, comply with applicable obligations, or establish, exercise or defend legal claims. Where longer retention is necessary, access and use are limited to that purpose.

Deleting bot records does not itself delete Instagram comments, conversations or Meta’s own records. Information retained within Instagram is subject to the relevant account handling and Meta’s retention rules.

You may object to legitimate-interest processing for reasons relating to your situation, and to direct marketing at any time. Where processing relies on consent, you may withdraw it without affecting earlier lawful processing.

For deletion, identify your Instagram username, which of our accounts you contacted, and approximate interaction dates so we can locate relevant records. You may complain to a data protection supervisory authority, particularly in the EU country of your habitual residence, workplace or the alleged infringement.

7. Automated replies and moderation

The bot can select template responses and hide or delete comments automatically using configured text rules. These functions are not intended to make decisions with legal or similarly significant effects under Article 22 GDPR. If a response or moderation action seems wrong, contact us at the email above to request human review.
